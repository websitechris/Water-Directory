## The accountability tracker — Chris's idea, 17 Aug 2026

**The gap:** headlines report the allegation, then nothing. Directors "face
charges", a company is "hit with a record fine" — and no outlet ever publishes
what actually happened. Follow the headline upstream.

**Format: a tracker, not a post.** A post about this ages in a week. Structured,
updated records don't:

```
Company · Date · What was alleged · Route (prosecuted / enforcement undertaking
/ Ofwat penalty) · Amount · Who received the money · Current status · Sources
```

Page type, not article. Same thing the site already does well.

### The mechanism has a name: Enforcement Undertakings

Verified 17 Aug 2026. A company voluntarily offers a payment to an environmental
charity and in exchange avoids prosecution. **Lawful** — a statutory mechanism.

The detail that makes it a story: **an enforcement undertaking bars any future
prosecution for that offence, including by third parties.** Once accepted, the
case is permanently closed.

Live right now:

- EA announced a **record £8.5m** secured via enforcement undertakings in 2025/26, press-released as *"Crackdown on water companies delivers record funding to restore harm done to waterways"*
- Channel 4 News investigated the same mechanism as companies **escaping** prosecution
- FOI data: EA downgraded **5,998 reported serious incidents to "minor" between 2016–2025 without visiting the site**
- EA published a blog post in **May 2026** responding to the media coverage

Same £8.5m, two opposite framings. That contrast is the article.

### ⚠️ Do not call it fraud

It is a lawful statutory process. Stating that a named company committed fraud
is defamation, and the accurate version is more damning anyway: this is legal,
documented, and it permanently removes the possibility of prosecution. "Legal"
lands harder than "fraud" because it survives a lawyer's read.

Same caution applies doubly to named individuals. Only claims traceable to a
court listing, regulator notice or official record.

### The original angle nobody has done

Enforcement undertaking payments go to **named** charities. Those names are
public. The **Charity Commission register** is free and searchable.

Cross-reference: which charities receive this money, how much, what share of
their income it represents, and whether any have industry connections. That is
public data, defensible, and the sort of work that earns links from people who
would never link to a water quality directory.

### Follow-up cases to track

- **Water (Special Measures) Act 2025** — made hiding evidence of illegal spills a criminal offence, up to 2 years for individuals. *Has anyone actually been prosecuted under it?* Check court listings and regulator notices. If the answer is nobody, that is itself the story.
- **Thames Water £123m Ofwat penalty** (announced 28 May 2025) — payment was restructured, with a backstop as late as 31 March 2030. Has any of it actually been paid?
- **EIR national-security refusals** — see `NEWS_SOURCES.md`. Chris has first-hand standing here.
