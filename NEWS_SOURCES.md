# Water industry news — where to actually get it

Companion to `BLOG_IDEAS.md`. That file is for capturing ideas. This one is for
verifying them before anything goes live.

**Rule:** AI summaries and news aggregators are for *finding* stories. Every
number, date, company name and fine amount gets confirmed against a primary
source below before publication. No exceptions.

> Worked example, Aug 2026: a summary stated Ofwat concluded its investigation
> and fined Thames Water £123m "in July 2026". The actual announcement was
> **28 May 2025** — the summary had conflated it with later payment-schedule
> news. The source it cited was even dated 2025. This is the normal failure
> mode, not an unusual one.

---

## Primary sources

### Regulators — fines, investigations, enforcement

| Source | What it gives you |
|---|---|
| **Ofwat enforcement register** — ofwat.gov.uk | Every fine and undertaking, with dates, amounts and the actual decision documents. The definitive record. |
| **EA Environmental Performance Assessment** (annual) | Star ratings 1–4 per company, serious pollution incident counts. Published ~July each year. Excellent for league-table content. |
| **EA Event Duration Monitoring returns** (annual, ~end March) | Spill counts per overflow. You already use this. |
| **Drinking Water Inspectorate** — annual reports + enforcement | Water quality events, undertakings against companies. Underused by media. |
| **Consumer Council for Water** | Complaints data by company. |

### Government and Parliament

| Source | What it gives you |
|---|---|
| **gov.uk / DEFRA announcements** | Policy, legislation, ministerial statements. Dated and citable. |
| **EFRA Select Committee** — publications.parliament.uk | Evidence sessions and reports. Witness transcripts contain quotes nobody else has mined. |
| **Hansard** | Exact wording of what ministers actually said, with date. |
| **legislation.gov.uk** | The Acts themselves — e.g. Water (Special Measures) Act 2025. Read the section, don't trust the summary of it. |

### Company financials — debt, dividends, gearing

| Source | What it gives you |
|---|---|
| **Company annual reports** (each company publishes) | Dividends, debt, gearing, executive pay. The real numbers behind "£72bn in dividends" claims. |
| **Companies House** | Corporate structure, parent entities, filings. Useful for "who actually owns this" pieces. |
| **Ofwat "Monitoring financial resilience"** (annual) | Gearing and credit ratings across the sector, from the regulator. |

### The EIR / transparency angle — see below, this is the interesting one

| Source | What it gives you |
|---|---|
| **ICO decision notices** — ico.org.uk | **Every** appeal decision, free, searchable, citable, with reference numbers. |
| **First-tier Tribunal (Information Rights)** decisions | Where ICO decisions get appealed. Full reasoning published. |
| **WhatDoTheyKnow** | Public FOI/EIR request archive — including refusals, with the full correspondence. |

## The story you're actually best placed to write

The national-security-exemption angle is genuinely strong, and not because it
makes a good outrage headline. Because **you have first-hand standing.**

You filed an EIR request with Thames Water for postcode-to-zone mapping. You
were blocked. That is not a story you are rewriting from the Telegraph — it is
something that happened to you, and you have the dataset to show what the gap
means in practice.

What makes it defensible:

1. **ICO decision notices are public and nobody has aggregated them.** Search
   for decisions involving water companies and Regulation 12(5)(a) — the
   national security exception under EIR 2004. Each notice sets out the exact
   argument the company made and whether the Commissioner accepted it. That is
   primary-source journalism sitting in a free public database.
2. **You can quantify the consequence.** You are the only person who can say
   "here is the coverage map with Thames Water missing, here is what that means
   for 15 million people trying to check their water" — because you built it.
3. **It ties to your own data.** Every other outlet writes the outrage. You can
   write the outrage *and* show the hole in the map.

**Route if you want to push the request itself:** request an Internal Review
from the company, then escalate to the ICO. Both steps generate public,
citable documents — and the process itself is the article.

## What not to do

**Don't rewrite national news.** Competing with the Telegraph, Guardian and BBC
on a Thames Water story means competing on their turf with none of their
authority. You will not rank, and rewriting their copy carries copyright risk.

**Do combine news with your data.** "Ofwat fined X £Nm — here is what X's spill
data actually looks like in your postcode" is a piece only you can write. That
is the format worth repeating.

**Watch the tone.** Sewage and privatisation are emotive, and outrage is the
easy play. The sewage-spills pages deliberately explain that rising spill counts
partly reflect better monitoring. Keep that standard in the blog. Being the
calm, accurate one is a positioning choice, and in this niche it is an open goal.

## Verification checklist — before any post goes live

- [ ] Every figure traced to a named primary source with a date
- [ ] Every fine/penalty confirmed on the Ofwat enforcement register
- [ ] Every legal claim checked against legislation.gov.uk, not a summary of it
- [ ] Dates stated explicitly ("announced 28 May 2025", not "recently")
- [ ] Company names exact (Thames Water Utilities Ltd vs Thames Water)
- [ ] Named individuals — extra care, and only claims you can source directly
- [ ] Links out to the primary sources (this builds trust and earns links back)
